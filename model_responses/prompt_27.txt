Solution: First, we characterize all ordered pairs of general (not necessarily odd) positive integers
(m, n) such that gcd(4m+ 2m+ 1,4n+ 2n+ 1)>1. We claim that ( m, n) works if and only if either
•mandnare both even, or
•ν3(m) =ν3(n).
Proof of necessity. Suppose that pis a prime that divides both 4m+ 2m+ 1 and 4n+ 2n+ 1. If
p= 3, mandnmust both be even. Henceforth assume that p̸= 3. Let dbe the order of 2 modulo
p. Then, as p|23m−1, we find that d|3m. Furthermore, we cannot have p|2m−1. Otherwise, by
lifting the exponent (and p̸= 3),
νp(23m−1) = νp(2m−1) +νp(3) = νp(2m−1) =⇒νp(4m+ 2m+ 1) = 0 .
The previous two results imply ν3(d) =ν3(m) + 1. Similarly, ν3(d) =ν3(n) + 1, so ν3(m) =ν3(n).
Proof of sufficiency. Ifmandnare both even, then 3 divides both 4m+ 2m+ 1 and 4n+ 2n+ 1.
Ifν3(m) =ν3(n) =k, then we claim that 43k+ 23k+ 1 divides both 4m+ 2m+ 1 and 4n+ 2n+ 1.
Indeed, note that as polynomials, x2+x+ 1 divides x2ℓ+xℓ+ 1 when 3 ∤ℓ. Plugging in x= 23kwith
ℓ=m/3kandℓ=n/3kyields the desired result.
